How Do You Know If an Employee Is an A, B, or C Player?

Vageesh Dwivedi • September 13, 2023 • 4 min read

Every engineering leader wants as many "A Players" as possible on their team. But when you look past the buzzwords, what does an A Player actually look like in day-to-day execution?

To accurately identify whether an engineer or team member is an A, B, or C player, I evaluate 4 core behavioral criteria:

  1. What happens when you delegate to them?
  2. What happens when you recruit?
  3. What happens when they need to do something new?
  4. What happens when they are blocked?

The Breakdown by Tier

🌟 A Players

  • When you delegate: You are 100% confident it will get done with excellence.
  • When you recruit: You actively search for candidates who share their caliber.
  • When faced with the unknown: They teach themselves and bring solutions.
  • When blocked: They proactively ask for help early with clear options.

⚖️ B Players

  • When you delegate: They get it mostly done, but require constant guidance and check-ins.
  • When you recruit: You always look for someone better to lift the average.
  • When faced with the unknown: You need to actively curate and guide their learning path.
  • When blocked: They burn valuable time struggling inefficiently rather than admitting they need assistance.

⚠️ C Players

  • When you delegate: You are worried it won't get done right, or will create technical debt.
  • When you recruit: You purposefully look for someone completely different.
  • When faced with the unknown: You must micromanage every step, and errors still occur.
  • When blocked: They rarely communicate, allowing deadlines to silently slip.

Management Takeaways

Set unambiguous definitions of success: A players thrive on defining the objectives collaboratively. B players need clear rails to execute with confidence. C players will find excuses why the targets were unrealistic from day one.
