One-on-One 101: Bringing Problems with a Point of View

Vageesh Dwivedi • April 5, 2024 • 3 min read

When you are struggling with a complex challenge or bottleneck at work, the most natural instinct is to flag it immediately in your upcoming 1-on-1 with your engineering manager.

While sharing your struggles with your manager is healthy and encouraged, how you bring the problem makes all the difference in your career growth and credibility:

  • If you only bring raw complaints: You inadvertently damage your credibility. Rather than being seen as a problem-solver, you risk being perceived as a complainer who expects others to do the heavy lifting.
  • If you cannot answer basic follow-up questions: You may come across as overwhelmed, unfocused, and lacking analytical depth.
  • If you articulate your thought process and perspective: You establish yourself as a strategic partner. Your manager can ask clarifying questions, challenge assumptions constructively, and collaborate with you to unlock solutions.

The 3-Step Framework for High-Impact 1-on-1s

  1. State the Problem Clearly: Define the root issue concisely without emotional hyperbole.
  2. Share Your Diagnosis: Explain why you believe the problem is occurring based on technical telemetry or team dynamics.
  3. Propose 2–3 Options with a Recommendation: "Option A accelerates delivery but incurs technical debt. Option B requires 3 more days of refactoring but protects our SLA. I recommend Option B because..."

Next time you walk into a 1-on-1, take 5 minutes before the meeting to articulate your thinking. Go in not with just a problem, but with a clear, reasoned point of view.
