"Fragmented-Dependent" Scrum Execution Model

Vageesh Dwivedi • July 2, 2022 • 5 min read

Though we began optimizing our scrum teams into this model in July 2020, by early 2021 we realized we had evolved an execution framework closely aligned with the LeSS (Large-Scale Scrum) principles, adapted specifically for high-velocity enterprise platforms with cross-geo dependencies.

Pod Structure

Instead of a single amorphous 20-person team where ceremonies take hours and context is scattered, we structured engineering into focused Pods:

  • Pod Lead: Ideally a Principal MTS, Tech Lead, or high-potential Senior MTS.
  • Pod Size: 3 to 5 engineers dedicated to coherent product focus areas.
  • Bandwidth-Based Delivery: If an Epic is estimated at 48 story points, a 3-engineer pod focuses on and delivers the complete feature over a 2-month horizon rather than fragmenting context across sprints.

Workflow & Ceremony Innovations

To eliminate meeting fatigue and increase estimation accuracy, we re-architected the traditional Scrum cadence:

1. Pre-Grooming Meeting: A weekly 30-minute sync between Product Owner, Engineering Owner, Scrum Master, and Pod Leads. The PO articulates user value, while Engineering Owners outline technical debt and architectural requirements before stories reach developers.

2. Engineering Brainstorming Meeting: Weekly technical whiteboarding session moderated by an engineering peer (not the manager) to collaboratively break down architecture, identify API interfaces, and create Jira action items.

3. Slotted Sprint Planning: Each pod joins planning in designated 15-to-20 minute slots rather than forcing the entire 20-person organization to sit through every unrelated item. Planning velocity improved by 300%.

4. The Rotating Support Baton: Production and E2E issue firefighting can paralyze feature velocity if distributed randomly. We introduced a rotating monthly "Support Pod" role. During their active month, this pod absorbs all production defects, patch releases, and SRE coordination. Other pods maintain 100% uninterrupted flow on feature milestones.

Long-Term Cultural Effects

  • True End-to-End Ownership: Engineers become accountable for the full customer journey rather than cherry-picking isolated tasks.
  • Asynchronous Standups: As maturity increases, synchronous status meetings are replaced with asynchronous channel check-ins.
  • Manager as Capability Enhancer: The engineering director steps away from task allocation and transitions into a force multiplier, optimizing policies, unblocking cross-team friction, and coaching talent.
