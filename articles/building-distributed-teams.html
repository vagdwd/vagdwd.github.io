Building Distributed Engineering Teams Across Geographies

Vageesh Dwivedi • June 8, 2018 • 6 min read

At the Engineering Leadership Meetup hosted at Instacart's San Francisco headquarters, leaders from GitHub, Instacart, and fast-growing remote-first startups gathered to break down what truly makes distributed engineering teams thrive.

Core Principles for Scaling Remote Organizations

When people do not trust each other to do the right thing across time zones, breakdown is inevitable. Building high-performing distributed teams requires intentional engineering culture:

  • Start Early: If you are building a company and want a distributed team, do it early. It is exponentially harder to retrofit remote culture into a centralized organization once habits solidify.
  • The In-Person Travel Requirement: Managers should see direct reports at least four times a year in person. Allocate real budget for regular all-hands and off-sites. Without physical face-to-face connection, social capital degrades.
  • Expressing Emotion Over Video: Jason Warner (former GitHub VP Eng) shared: "When I am thinking on camera, I tend to look down and my smile disappears. I've learned to actively express more body language and emotion over VC so people don't misinterpret intense focus as disapproval."
  • Radical Context Transparency: You must give engineers all relevant strategic context. When leaders withhold information, rumor mills spin up and people invent their own narrative. Over-communicate business objectives until you feel you cannot say them one more time.

Anti-Patterns to Eliminate

1. Ego & Jerkiness: It is remarkably easy to come across as abrasive over Slack and asynchronous PR comments. Cultivate the habit of assuming best intent.

2. Information Silos: Distributed teams fail when HQ keeps decision-making behind closed doors. Every architectural RFC and product decision must be documented publicly within the company.
