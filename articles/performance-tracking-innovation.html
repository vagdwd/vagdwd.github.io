How We Transformed Performance Tracking with a Simple Innovation

Vageesh Dwivedi • February 20, 2025 • 5 min read

Everyone knows about the S.M.A.R.T. goals framework introduced in 1981 in paper by George T. Doran. In most organizations, goal management is tightly linked to performance management. Ideally, goals are set, cascaded top-down, and reviewed periodically. However, the reality often diverges from this simple and structured approach. Employees and managers tend to update performance evaluation systems only at the end of the appraisal cycle, leading to problems such as:

  • Employees forget key accomplishments
  • Managerial bias towards recent events (recency effect)
  • Challenges in tracking contributions across multiple projects
  • Lack of continuous feedback, especially in remote work settings
  • Employee updates becoming overly generic, often stating “All goals on track” or as brief as “All good,” limiting the usefulness of the data collected. ()

During the pandemic, these issues became more pronounced. HR teams attempted various interventions, from email reminders to leadership messaging, but engagement remained low.

The Turning Point: Leveraging Microsoft Teams “Updates”

While exploring Microsoft Teams, I discovered the “Updates” tool (for usage see Microsoft Learn). Recognizing its potential, I initiated an experiment where employees filled out a simple form every month, answering six key questions:

  1. Which objectives in OKRs or Epics did you work on this month? (Provide links.)
  2. Beyond OKRs or Epics, what else did you contribute to? (Describe results and individual contributions.)
  3. What was your best work highlight this month? (Explain why it stood out.)
  4. What was a lowlight? (Reflect on what could have been done better.)
  5. What unplanned work did you take on? (E.g., technical debt repayments.)
  6. Who had the most impact on your work this month? (Express gratitude. )

There was an explicit option to write NONE for responses, which was a subtle attempt toward self-realization for employees.

This data was collected through a structured process:

  • Employees submitted responses in the MS Teams “Updates” app.
  • Managers reviewed and discussed responses in a dedicated thread.
  • An Excel macro converted this data into text files for easy HR system updates. (This could be automated by powerautomate feature in office 365, if your plan allows that)
  • HR business partners were informed of this modification.

The Results: A Game-Changer for Performance Tracking

This simple yet structured approach yielded phenomenal results:

  • 85% adoption rate, compared to less than 30% for the HR system.
  • Four key insights captured: Accomplishments, Alignment, Interruptions, and Gratitude Exchange.
  • Real-time tracking: Managers could see timeseries and aggregate trends.
  • Improved decision-making: Managers had better visibility into team performance, blockers, and enablers.

Upon the success of this experiment, the process was shared with other teams for review and potential adoption, highlighting the benefits and observed results.

Important Caveat

The claim here is not that this process is the best performance measurement tool. There are much more sophisticated tools like Jellyfish, Linear8, Faros.ai, sleuth.io, DX and frameworks like DORA and SPACE (Detailed article at pragmatic engineer blog). However, for teams looking for a lightweight, easy-to-implement solution, this approach proved to be highly effective.

Key Takeaways

✅ Continuous feedback beats last-minute evaluations – Frequent updates prevent recency bias and forgotten achievements.

✅ Simplicity drives adoption – A user-friendly process encourages participation.

✅ Technology can solve process inefficiencies – Leveraging existing tools reduces manual workload.

✅ Recognition matters – Highlighting individual and team contributions fosters a positive culture.

✅ Data-driven insights improve management – Regular tracking helps align goals and address challenges proactively.

By leveraging an existing tool in a new way, we enhanced performance tracking with minimal disruption. Sometimes, innovation isn’t about reinventing the wheel—it’s about using what you already have, but more effectively.
